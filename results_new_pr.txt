Comparison with existing EEG emotion classification frameworks:
- Traditional shallow models such as SVM, k-NN, and Logistic Regression are widely used.
  • SVM often performs well on high-dimensional EEG features but is more sensitive to parameter tuning.
  • k-NN is simple but scales poorly and is sensitive to noisy features.
- Deep learning approaches (CNNs, RNN/LSTM, 1D-CNN on raw signals) can capture temporal and spatial patterns,
  but they require larger datasets, more compute, and careful regularization.
- In this work, tree-based ensemble methods are used:
  • Random Forest: robust to noise, gives good baseline with interpretability via feature importance.
  • XGBoost: gradient-boosted trees, often superior on tabular EEG features, handles non-linear boundaries well.
  • Our comparison shows which of these two ensembles is empirically best for the given feature set and labels.
